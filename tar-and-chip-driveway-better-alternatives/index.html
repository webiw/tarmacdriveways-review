In Dublin, many homeowners search for a tar and chip driveway because it seems like a cost effective alternative to more familiar paving materials. 
As local driveway specialists, we’ve seen how this chip surface performs in Ireland’s wet climate and why it isn’t always the best option. 
This article explains what a tar and chip surface is, why we don’t install it, and which surfaces – like tarmac and asphalt – offer longer life and minimal upkeep for your home.

What is a Tar and Chip Driveway?

 

A tar and chip surface is sometimes called a chip seal or chip tar.

The installation process uses hot liquid tar sprayed over a prepared gravel base. Loose stone chips are then rolled into the tar with a tar sprayer, creating a textured finish.

Homeowners can choose different stone colours and chip finishes to personalise the look.

Because the materials are simple – hot bitumen, stone chip and loose stone chips – the surface is inexpensive and can be laid quickly, even over an existing surface. You might see chip driveways on private roadways or large driveways in rural areas where the rustic appearance blends into the landscape.

At first glance, the benefits are appealing: this tar and chip surface costs around €25 per square metre, offers good traction in wet weather and hides oil spills better than concrete.

However, as we explain below, its lifespan and maintenance make it unsuitable for most Dublin homes.

Why We Don’t Recommend Tar and Chip in Dublin

 

Dublin’s weather – frequent rain, occasional frost and the odd heatwave – puts a lot of stress on driveways.

A tar and chip driveway usually lasts only 8 to 10 years, sometimes as little as 5 years if the driveway sees heavier traffic.

The loose stones tend to work free over time, especially on slopes or when turning wheels. To keep the surface safe you must top up the chips regularly. Clearing snow or moss is also a challenge: snowploughs or stiff brushes can damage the chip surface and require expensive repairs.

Another problem is finding skilled installers.

Unlike asphalt or tarmac, tar and chip is not a DIY-friendly job and only a few Irish contractors offer it.

In our experience, most clients come to us after the disappointment of chip driveways tar – they find weeds growing between chips, the surface washing into drains, and the driveway looking messy after a few winters.

That is why we no longer provide tar and chip in Dublin.

  • Short lifespan (7‑15 years) and more prone to potholes.
  • Loose stones require constant re-chipping and sweep up.
  • Hard to clear snow, moss or weeds without damaging the surface.
  • Very few local contractors have the equipment for laying tar and chip.
  • Not suitable for heavy vehicles or steep slopes.

Better Alternatives – Tarmac and Asphalt Driveways

 

If you want a surface that stands up to Dublin’s rain and occasional frost, tarmac and asphalt are much more reliable.

Both are created by mixing aggregate with hot liquid asphalt or bitumen and laying it with specialised machinery. When compacted, they form a smooth, durable surface that can last 20 years or more with minimal upkeep.

  • Tarmac driveway: This popular choice is quick to install and cost effective. It cures within hours, resists water penetration and can be laid over an existing driveway with proper preparation. Regular sealing every few years helps extend the lifespan.
  • Asphalt driveway: Asphalt uses a denser mix of aggregate and bitumen. It costs more per square metre but lasts longer and provides a sleek, modern finish. Our guide on the differences between tarmac and asphalt explains why asphalt is ideal for busy family homes and car parks.
  • SMA (Stone Mastic Asphalt): For heavy traffic or steep gradients, SMA combines coarse stone with a strong binder. It withstands Irish weather, resists cracking and offers non-slip texture. It’s perfect for large driveways or commercial properties.
  • Resin-bound surfaces: If you want a permeable, eco-friendly option, a resin-bound driveway binds coloured stones in a clear resin. The surface is smooth but allows water to drain, reducing puddles.
  • Concrete driveways: Concrete is extremely durable and can last 40–50 years. Our article on concrete driveways explains how they handle heavy vehicles, require little maintenance and can be stamped or coloured for a bespoke look.

All of these alternatives offer a durable surface with less maintenance than tar and chip. They are suitable for both small front gardens and large driveways, and they comply with local regulations regarding drainage and runoff. For eco-conscious homeowners, eco-friendly tarmac driveways use recycled materials and are designed to reduce environmental impact.

Cost and Lifespan Comparison

 

Cost is often the main reason people consider chip tar drives. A chip drive typically costs about €35 per m².

A standard tarmac driveway costs from around €60–€80 per m² while asphalt may range from €70–€90 per m² depending on the finish.

SMA costs slightly more again. When you factor in lifespan, the value difference becomes clear: tar and chip lasts roughly 8 to 10 years, tarmac about 15–20 years, and asphalt or SMA can last over two decades.

Concrete lasts even longer, often 40 years, but requires a bigger upfront investment.

A longer lifespan means fewer replacements and less disruption to your daily life.

Case Study – Replacing a Tar and Chip Driveway in Tallaght

 

We recently met a homeowner in Tallaght who had a 70 m² chip driveway on a slight slope. Installed eight years ago, the tar and chip surface looked tired. Loose stones had washed into the drains, moss was growing through the chip finish, and the driveway installation hadn’t included proper edging. The client was spending money every year on maintenance tar and re-chipping. After discussing options, she chose to upgrade to asphalt.

Our Tallaght tarmac contractor removed the existing surface and installed a new asphalt drive with drainage channels. The installation process took three days, including removal, new sub-base, laying hot bitumen and compacting the hot liquid asphalt. The new drive cost about €85 per m² (€6,000 total), but it will last at least 20 years. The result was a smooth, dark surface that complemented the home, improved kerb appeal and required only occasional sweeping and sealing. Our client was delighted with the minimal upkeep and commented that the investment was worthwhile compared to constant chip repairs.

Step-by-Step Guide to Choosing the Right Driveway

If you’re unsure which surface suits your home, use this simple checklist:

  • Assess your current surface: Is your existing driveway sound? A solid base means you can overlay with tarmac or asphalt without removing all layers.
  • Consider traffic and use: Light residential use may cope with gravel driveway or chip finishes, but families with multiple cars or heavy vans need a robust surface.
  • Evaluate the slope and drainage: Steeper drives may need textured SMA or resin-bound surfaces and an effective drainage solution.
  • Compare materials: Read our guides on the differences between tarmac and asphalt, concrete and resin-bound to see which fits your budget, style and maintenance expectations.
  • Get a quote: Use our tarmac driveway cost calculator or contact our team for a personalised quote. Reviewing recent tarmac projects and the Dundrum project can inspire your design.
  • Choose an experienced contractor: Check references and ensure your installer is licensed and insured. Our team of contractors covers every area from Terenure to Swords and offers free consultations.

Conclusion

While a tar and chip driveway seems attractive because of its low upfront cost and rustic charm, our Dublin climate makes it less practical. Frequently Asked Questions About Tar and Chip Driveway

What is a tar and chip driveway?

It is a layered surface where hot tar (bitumen) is applied over a gravel base and covered with loose stone chips. The chips are rolled into the tar, creating a textured finish.

Why isn’t tar and chip popular in Dublin?

Our wet and sometimes frosty climate shortens its lifespan. Loose stones wash away, and snow removal or moss cleaning can damage the surface. There are also very few skilled contractors offering chip driveways in the city.

How does the lifespan compare with tarmac?

A tar and chip surface usually lasts from 8 to 10 years and sometimes less than 5 years with heavy traffic. A tarmac or asphalt driveway can last 15–20 years or more with proper maintenance.

What are the cost differences between tar and chip and tarmac/asphalt?

Tar and chip costs roughly €35 per square metre. Tarmac typically ranges from €60–€80 per square metre, while asphalt may cost €70–€90 per square metre. The longer lifespan of tarmac and asphalt often makes them more cost effective over time.

What maintenance does a tar and chip driveway need?

Regular sweeping to remove loose stones, periodic re-chipping to replace lost aggregate, and careful snow removal to avoid damage. Over time it may need a fresh layer of hot bitumen and chips.

Are there eco-friendly alternatives?

Yes. Resin-bound and eco-friendly tarmac surfaces use recycled materials and allow water to drain naturally. They reduce runoff and are ideal for sustainable home improvements.

Which surface is best for Irish weather?

For Dublin’s changeable weather, tarmac, asphalt or SMA provide reliable performance. They resist rain, frost and heavy use better than tar and chip, making them the best surfaces for Irish climates.
